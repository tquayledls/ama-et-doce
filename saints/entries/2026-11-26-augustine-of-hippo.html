St. Augustine of Hippo

Bishop and Doctor of the Church. Memorial: August 28[1]

Painting

Saint Augustine, Philippe de Champaigne, c. 1645–50, Los Angeles County Museum of Art[2]

The Essentials

  • Origin: Tagaste, a small free city of proconsular Numidia in North Africa (now Souk-Ahras). His father, Patricius, a town councillor, was still a pagan; his mother, Monica, was a Christian.[3][4]
  • Born: November 13, 354.[3]
  • Died: August 28, 430, at Hippo, in the seventy-sixth year of his age, of an illness that struck early in the Vandal siege of the city.[5]
  • Baptized: by St. Ambrose at Milan, on Easter Day or during Eastertide, 387.[6] Ordained priest: 391. Bishop of Hippo: 396 to 430.[7][8]
  • Doctor of the Church: one of the four great Western Doctors of the early Middle Ages, with Gregory the Great, Ambrose and Jerome.[9]
  • Feast: August 28, a memorial in the General Roman Calendar. Today is an open day, and his own feast is more than 30 days away.[1]

His Life in Brief

  • His mother signed him with the cross and enrolled him among the catechumens as a boy, but he put off baptism.[10]
  • At about nineteen he read Cicero's Hortensius, which gave him a love of wisdom; he then fell in with the Manichæans, who promised a scientific explanation of nature.[11][12]
  • He went to Italy in 383 and became a regular hearer of Bishop Ambrose at Milan.[13]
  • In September 386, at thirty-three, a "grand stroke of grace" smote him to the ground in the garden at Milan, and a few days later he left his professorship.[14]
  • Ordained priest at Hippo in 391, he founded a monastery there; the people had cheered him and begged Bishop Valerius to ordain him.[15]
  • As bishop he defended the faith against the Manichæans, Donatists and Pelagians until his death in the siege of Hippo.[16][5]

Famous Excerpt: by Augustine

From the Confessions, Book X, ch. 27:

"Too late did I love You, O Fairness, so ancient, and yet so new! Too late did I love You! For behold, You were within, and I without, and there did I seek You; I, unlovely, rushed heedlessly among the things of beauty You made. You were with me, but I was not with You."[17]

1. Catechesis

Lesson: we were made for God, and nothing less will rest the heart. The first lines of the Confessions give the whole Catechism in a sentence: "You have made us for Yourself, and our hearts are restless until they rest in You."[18] His own road to that rest ran through a mother's prayers, the books of the philosophers and a bishop's sermons.[10][13]

Exercise: today, when you notice a restless want, ask what you are really reaching for.

2. Apologetics

Objection: "Evil proves there is no good God," or "Man is not free." Augustine took this objection from the inside; he had been a Manichæan, one of those who explained evil as a second, equal principle.[12] As a priest and bishop he answered them, and the Catholic Encyclopedia says his success was "prodigious."[19] He defended free will against the Manichæans, and he defended the Church against the Donatist schism.[20][21]

3. Theology

  • Grace: against Pelagius he taught that "even these first desires for salvation are due to the grace of God."[22]
  • The Church: the Catholic Encyclopedia says that, in the view of one scholar, Augustine "deserves to be named the Doctor of the Church as well as the Doctor of Grace."[23]
  • His estimate: Möhler, quoted by the same encyclopedia: "For depth of feeling and power of conception nothing written on the Church since St. Paul's time, is comparable to the works of St. Augustine."[24]

4. Devotional Prayer

Collect, Memorial of St. Augustine (Roman Missal, August 28):[25]
Renew in your Church, we pray, O Lord, that spirit with which you endowed your Bishop Saint Augustine that, filled with the same spirit, we may thirst for you, the sole fount of true wisdom, and seek you, the author of heavenly love. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever.

Practice: Pray the Collect once today, and read slowly the lines from the Confessions above.

Today's Takeaway

The heart is restless until it rests in God; seek Him within, where He was waiting all along.[17][18]

St. Augustine of Hippo, pray for us.
