St. Rose Philippine Duchesne

Virgin, Religious of the Sacred Heart and Missionary. Optional Memorial: November 18

Painting

Portrait of St. Rose Philippine Duchesne, anonymous, Society of the Sacred Heart (public-domain reproduction)[1]

The Essentials

  • Origin: born in Grenoble, France, into a family among the new rich; she learned political skill from her father and love of the poor from her mother.[2] [3]
  • Born: August 29, 1769.[4]
  • Died: November 18, 1852, at the age of 83, after thirty-four years of extending the work of the Society of the Sacred Heart.[4]
  • Beatified and canonized: beatified in 1940 and canonized in 1988.[4]
  • Feast: November 18, an optional memorial in the U.S. calendar.[5]

Her Life in Brief

Rose's temperament was a strong and dauntless will, which became the material, and the battlefield, of her holiness.[3]

  • She entered the Visitation of Mary convent at nineteen, and stayed despite her family's opposition.[6] (The Society's own account says seventeen, and that the Visitation nuns at Sainte Marie d'en Haut had educated her.[4])
  • When the French Revolution closed the convent, she cared for the poor and sick, opened a school for homeless children, and risked her life helping priests in the underground.[7]
  • In 1804 she persuaded Mother Madeleine Sophie Barat, founder of the Society of the Sacred Heart, to take over the convent of Sainte Marie, and she was professed in 1805.[4]
  • In 1818, at forty-nine, she sailed with four companions for America. They spent eleven weeks at sea to New Orleans and seven more on the Mississippi to St. Louis.[8] [4]
  • At St. Charles, Missouri, she opened the first free school for girls west of the Mississippi.[9]
  • At seventy-one she resigned as superior, and at seventy-two went to the Potawatomi mission at Sugar Creek. She never learned their language, and they named her "Woman-Who-Prays-Always."[4] [10]

Famous Excerpt: by Philippine

During an all-night vigil before the Blessed Sacrament on Holy Thursday, she prayed to be sent to America. Her account of it:

"All night long I was in the New World, and I traveled in good company. First of all I reverently gathered up all the Precious Blood from the Garden, the Praetorium, and Calvary. Then I took possession of our Lord in the Blessed Sacrament. Holding him close to my heart, I went forth to scatter my treasure everywhere, without fear that it would be exhausted."[11]

1. Catechesis

Lesson: every Christian shares in Christ's mission to make him known. The Collect for her memorial says God "gave her the desire to make you known among all peoples," and asks the same love and zeal for all who honor her. The Society's preferred Gospel for the day is John 12:20-26.[12] [13]

Exercise: read John 12:20-26 once, and name one person to whom you can make Christ known this week, by word or by prayer.

2. Apologetics

Objection: "A missionary who cannot even speak the language accomplishes nothing." Philippine reached the Potawatomi at seventy-two and could not learn their speech. While others taught, she prayed, and the people named her "Woman-Who-Prays-Always."[10] The Society records that her "contemplative presence" was so evident that the Indians gave her that name.[4] Her witness was prayer, and they recognized it.

3. Theology

  • The Precious Blood and the Blessed Sacrament: in her vigil she gathered the Blood of Christ from the Garden, the Praetorium and Calvary, and carried the Lord in the Blessed Sacrament "close to my heart" to scatter it everywhere.[11]
  • The Heart of Jesus: she gave her life to the Society of the Sacred Heart, and the Society's alternative opening prayer says she drew her generosity "from the Heart of Jesus."[4] [14]
  • Perseverance in disappointment: the bishop had no place for a mission to Native Americans, and sent her to "the remotest village in the U.S."; she began there with courage and founded a free school.[15]

4. Devotional Prayer

Collect, Memorial of St. Rose Philippine Duchesne (Roman Missal, November 18):[12]
Gracious God, you filled the heart of Philippine Duchesne with charity and missionary zeal, and gave her the desire to make you known among all peoples. Fill us who honor her memory today, with that same love and zeal to extend your kingdom to the ends of the earth. We ask this through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever.

Entrance verse, Memorial of Rose Philippine Duchesne (Isaiah 52:7):[16]
How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of those who bring good news.

Practice: Pray the Collect once today, and spend a few minutes in silent prayer for someone who does not yet know Christ.

Today's Takeaway

Philippine gave up a comfortable life to carry Christ to a new world, and in the end her prayer spoke where her words could not.[12] [10]

St. Rose Philippine Duchesne, pray for us.
