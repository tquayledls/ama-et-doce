St. Gertrude the Great

Benedictine Nun and Mystic. Optional Memorial: November 16

Painting

Santa Gertrudis, Miguel Cabrera, 1763, Dallas Museum of Art[1]

The Essentials

  • Origin: born in Germany; nothing is known of her family, not even her parents' names.[2] [3]
  • Born: January 6, 1256.[3]
  • Died: at Helfta, near Eisleben in Saxony, November 17, 1301 or 1302.[4]
  • Feast: November 16, an optional memorial in the Roman calendar.[5]
  • Patronage: declared Patroness of the West Indies.[6]

Her Life in Brief

When she was five years old, Gertrude entered the alumnate (the school) of the monastery of Helfta.[7]

  • She was entrusted to St. Mechtilde, mistress of the alumnate, and the two became close friends.[8]
  • In her twenty-sixth year she received the first of a series of visions that lasted to the end of her life.[9]
  • From then on, in her biographer's words, she "from being a grammarian became a theologian," leaving secular studies for Scripture, the Fathers and theology, and she copied, translated and wrote for the good of others.[10]
  • Outwardly she lived as a simple Benedictine nun; her charity embraced rich and poor, learned and simple, and she was tender toward the souls in purgatory.[11] [12]
  • She died at Helfta in 1301 or 1302.[4]

Famous Excerpt: by Gertrude

"Though I was hurrying to my eternal loss, your thoughts of me were thoughts of peace and not of affliction, and you lifted me up with so many great favors."[13]

These words come from the Office of Readings for her memorial, taken from her Revelations (Legatus divinae pietatis, Book 2). Book 2 is the part written by the saint herself.[13] [14]

1. Catechesis

Lesson: God dwells in the heart that is open to him. The Collect for her memorial prays that we may "joyfully experience" God "present and at work within us," and the Mass reading is Paul's prayer that "Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith."[15] [16]

The Catholic Encyclopedia names devotion to the Sacred Heart as the characteristic of her piety: the symbol of the charity that moved the Word to take flesh, to institute the Eucharist, and to offer himself on the Cross.[17]

Exercise: read Ephesians 3:14-19 slowly once, and ask where in your day Christ is already dwelling.

2. Apologetics

Objection: "Study gets in the way of prayer." Gertrude knew the danger. The Catholic Encyclopedia says her love of study had been a hindrance to contemplation, yet it had kept her from more serious failings; she turned her scholarship to the Scriptures and the Fathers, and so made study serve prayer.[10] [18]

3. Theology

  • Her writings: the Herald of Divine Love (Legatus divinae pietatis), in five books, and the seven Exercises, which run from the reception of baptismal grace to the preparation for death.[14] [19]
  • Sources of her thought: her language is steeped in the liturgy and Scripture, and her spirit rests on the Rule of St. Benedict.[19] [20]
  • Tested by theologians: the superiors of Helfta had her works examined by Dominican and Franciscan theologians.[21]

4. Devotional Prayer

Collect, Optional Memorial of St. Gertrude the Great (Roman Missal, November 16):[15]
O God, who prepared a delightful dwelling for yourself in the heart of the Virgin Saint Gertrude, graciously bring light, through her intercession, to the darkness of our hearts, that we may joyfully experience you present and at work within us. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever.

Responsory, Office of Readings (see Jeremiah 31:3; Hosea 2:14-21):[22]
The Lord loved Gertrude with an everlasting love; from her childhood, he drew her to himself and led her into the wilderness. There he spoke tenderly to her. He espoused her to himself for ever in faith and in love.

Practice: Pray the Collect once today, and sit a minute with the line "your thoughts of me were thoughts of peace."

Today's Takeaway

Gertrude let God make a dwelling in her heart, and she gave her learning to him.[15] [10]

St. Gertrude the Great, pray for us.
