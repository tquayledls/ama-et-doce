St. Thomas More

Martyr, lawyer, Lord Chancellor of England, and patron of statesmen and politicians. Optional Memorial (with St. John Fisher): June 22[1]

Painting

Sir Thomas More, Hans Holbein the Younger, 1527, Frick Collection, New York[2]

The Essentials

  • Origin: London, the sole surviving son of Sir John More, a barrister and later a judge.[3]
  • Born: February 7, 1477 or 1478, in London. The Catholic Encyclopedia gives both years.[4]
  • Died: July 6, 1535, beheaded on Tower Hill in London. His body was buried in the Church of St. Peter ad Vincula.[5] He was found guilty of high treason at Westminster Hall.[6]
  • Beatified and canonized: beatified by Pope Leo XIII in 1886, together with St. John Fisher and other martyrs; canonized by Pope Pius XI in 1935.[7][8]
  • Feast: June 22, an optional memorial in the General Roman Calendar, shared with St. John Fisher.[1]

His Life in Brief

  • He lived for a time near the London Charterhouse and shared the prayer of the monks, and he weighed a vocation to the priesthood.[9] Erasmus, his friend, wrote that More could not shake off the desire for marriage and chose "to be a chaste husband rather than an impure priest."[10]
  • He married Jane Colt in 1505, and they had four children; after she died in 1511 he married Alice Middleton, a widow. The Church holds him up as an affectionate and faithful husband and father.[11]
  • He lectured on St. Augustine's The City of God to learned men in London.[12]
  • In October 1529 he succeeded Cardinal Wolsey as Lord Chancellor of England, a post never before held by a layman.[13] In May 1532 he resigned, after holding it less than three years.[14]
  • In April 1534 he refused to take the oath required by the Act of Succession, which also repudiated "any foreign authority," and a few days later he was sent to the Tower.[15][16]
  • In the Tower he wrote A Dialogue of Comfort Against Tribulation, and when his books and pens were taken away he went on writing letters with a charred stick or a piece of coal.[17][18]

Famous Excerpt: about More

When the Duke of Norfolk warned him with the Latin proverb indignatio principis mors est ("the anger of the prince is death"), the Catholic Encyclopedia records More's answer:

"Is that all, my Lord ... then, in good faith, between your grace and me is but this, that I shall die today, and you tomorrow."[19]

1. Catechesis

Lesson: a Christian is one person in every room. More was a husband, a father, a lawyer and a statesman, and he did not live a separate religious life on the side. Pope John Paul II, in naming him patron of statesmen, said that his life showed a "perfect harmony between faith and action."[20]

Exercise: today, pick one decision at work or at home and ask whether you would make it the same way if only God were watching.

2. Apologetics

Objection: "Faith has no place in public life; keep it private." More's answer was to serve the king faithfully in public, and to refuse the one thing that would have denied God. Pope Benedict XVI recalled that he was admired by believers and non-believers alike for following his conscience, "even at the cost of displeasing the sovereign whose 'good servant' he was, because he chose to serve God first."[21] The Church does not claim he was perfect: John Paul II notes that in his actions against heretics More "reflected the limits of the culture of his time."[22]

3. Theology

  • Conscience: John Paul II said that More's life speaks "of the inalienable dignity of the human conscience."[23]
  • Politics and morality: John Paul II said that what enlightened More's conscience was the sense that "man cannot be sundered from God, nor politics from morality."[24]
  • Patron of statesmen: in 2000 John Paul II declared him "the heavenly Patron of Statesmen and Politicians."[25]
  • Martyrdom as witness: the Collect asks that we may "confirm by the witness of our life the faith we profess with our lips."[26]

4. Devotional Prayer

Collect, Optional Memorial of Sts. John Fisher and Thomas More (Roman Missal, June 22):[26]
O God, who in martyrdom have brought true faith to its highest expression, graciously grant that, strengthened through the intercession of Saints John Fisher and Thomas More, we may confirm by the witness of our life the faith we profess with our lips. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever.

Practice: Pray the Collect once today, and name one place where your faith needs to show in your work.

Today's Takeaway

Serve well in the world, and serve God first; "confirm by the witness of our life the faith we profess with our lips."[26]

St. Thomas More, pray for us.
