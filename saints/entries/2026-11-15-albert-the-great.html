St. Albert the Great

Dominican Friar, Bishop, Doctor of the Church. Optional Memorial: November 15

Painting

Albertus Magnus, Tommaso da Modena, fresco, 1352, Treviso[1]

The Essentials

  • Origin: eldest son of the Count of Bollstädt, born at Lauingen in Swabia.[2] [3]
  • Born: c. 1205 or 1206, though many historians give 1193.[3]
  • Died: at Cologne, November 15, 1280.[4]
  • Feast: November 15, a Memorial of the Bishop and Doctor of the Church.[5]
  • Titles: called "the Great" and Doctor Universalis (Universal Doctor); beatified in 1622; canonized in 1931.[6] [7] [8]

His Life in Brief

Albert studied at Padua, and in 1223 joined the Order of St. Dominic.[9]

  • He received the doctor's degree at the University of Paris, and among his hearers was St. Thomas Aquinas.[10] [11]
  • In 1260 he was appointed Bishop of Ratisbon (Regensburg), and governed the diocese until 1262, when he returned to teaching at Cologne.[12] [13]
  • He took an active part in the Council of Lyons in 1274.[14]
  • When news came of Aquinas's death, he declared that "The Light of the Church" had been extinguished.[15]
  • In 1277 he journeyed to Paris to defend the memory of his disciple.[16]
  • He died at Cologne on November 15, 1280.[4]

Famous Excerpt: by Albert, and about him

"Whoever believes that Aristotle was a god, must also believe that he never erred. But if one believe that Aristotle was a man, then doubtless he was liable to error just as we are."[17]

The Catholic Encyclopedia gives this line from Albert's Physics commentary. It also cites his rule for natural science: "The aim of natural science is not simply to accept the statements of others, but to investigate the causes that are at work in nature."[18]

1. Catechesis

Lesson: the Eucharist is the Lord's own gift of himself. In the Office of Readings for his memorial, Albert comments on the words "Do this in remembrance of me": the sacrament is a command to be obeyed and a remembrance of the Lord's going to death for us.[19]

Exercise: read Albert's sentence below slowly once, then ask what it means that love "gives itself as food."

2. Apologetics

Objection: "Faith and science are enemies." Albert answered with his life. He wrote a treatise against the Averroists on the unity of the intellect in 1256, and set out to purify the works of Aristotle of error so that they could serve the faith.[20] [21] Roger Bacon and Albert proved to the world that the Church is not opposed to the study of nature, and that faith and science may go hand in hand.[22]

3. Theology

  • A universal scholar: his works range from logic and the physical sciences to theology and Scripture commentary.[6]
  • Master of Aquinas: he taught Thomas at Paris and Cologne, and the Catholic Encyclopedia says that under God we owe the Summa of St. Thomas to Albert.[11] [23]

4. Devotional Prayer

Collect, Memorial of St. Albert the Great (Roman Missal, November 15):[24]
O God, who made the Bishop Saint Albert great by his joining of human wisdom to divine faith, grant, we pray, that we may so adhere to the truths he taught, that through progress in learning we may come to a deeper knowledge and love of you. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever.

From Albert's commentary on Luke (Office of Readings):[25]
It is characteristic of the greatest love to give itself as food.

Practice: Pray the Collect once today before study or work, and sit a minute with Albert's sentence.

Today's Takeaway

Albert joined human wisdom to divine faith, and taught his pupil Thomas to do the same.[24] [23]

St. Albert the Great, pray for us.
